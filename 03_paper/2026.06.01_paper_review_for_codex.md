## What the paper is

*Minority Report: Contrarian Traders, Prediction Markets, and the Return of Post-Earnings Drift* (`03_paper/2026.06.01_main.tex`, 33 pp) studies Polymarket earnings beat/miss markets against IBES analyst consensus. It makes four claims: (#1) the crowd is well calibrated (Brier/BSS); (#2) order-flow imbalance predicts the beat/miss outcome; (#3) a short-only strategy on low crowd-assigned beat probability earns a tradeable return that is largely market-beta rather than factor-neutral alpha; (#4) on-chain "smart-money" wallets are skilled and persist out-of-sample. Primary sample is 340 markets (Sept 2025–Feb 2026); a disjoint 321-market set (with a 115-market post-Feb-18 holdout) and a pooled 661-market sample provide out-of-sample and power tests.

**Do not edit `main.tex` (the original) or `2026.06.01_main.tex` (the working draft).** Your output is a review document, not edits.

## The "consistent methodology" the numbers must obey

Numerical accuracy has to be judged against this methodology, applied uniformly:

1. **Beat/miss outcome** (#1 calibration, #2 flow, #4 wallets): primary 340 uses clean IBES `qtr_eps > eps_target`; the 321 set uses the on-chain Dome resolution (`resolved_beat`).
2. **Actual EPS magnitude** (walk-down, implied EPS, crowd-vs-consensus disagreement): clean IBES **quarterly** actuals only (`pdicity='QTR'`). yfinance and Compustat are deliberately dropped. The annual/quarterly contamination that this fixes is described in `2026.06.01_implied_eps_dilemma_for_codex.md` (companion brief).
3. **Consensus** is the market line (slug `eps_target`), which is statistically indistinguishable from the IBES consensus mean.
4. **Returns / strategy / four-cell / FF3 (all of them)**: anchor `t0` on the first trading day after the actual IBES announcement date (`anndats`), falling back to the slug earnings date when `anndats` is missing; drop look-ahead BMO events whose last prediction-market trade is not strictly before `anndats`; winsorize excess returns at 2/98; short-strategy return is the negative of the SPY-excess return; thresholds 0.20/0.25/0.30/0.35; horizons 1/5/10 trading days.
5. **Samples**: 340 primary; 321 out-of-sample; 661 pooled (the two are disjoint, with zero shared ticker-quarters, so pooling is valid).
6. **Framing already decided**: #3 is a robust short-side *return* that is marginal/beta-driven in risk-adjusted terms, not factor-neutral alpha. The walk-down dollar bias is ≈0 (the circulated +$1.55 intercept was an annual-vs-quarterly artifact). Implied EPS is reframed as a real crowd-vs-line *disagreement* plus a dollar-*accuracy null* (the crowd does not beat consensus as a point estimate once actuals are quarterly).

## Job 2 — readability and straightforwardness of language

Read the draft as a referee would. The author has deliberately moved to first-person plural ("we"), removed em-dashes, and cut casual diction; check that the result is clear, not stilted, and internally consistent.

1. **Clarity of the four findings.** Are #1–#4 stated the same way in the abstract, the introduction, the body, and the conclusion? Flag any place where the abstract or intro overclaims relative to what the tables show (especially #3: the abstract/intro should not imply factor-neutral alpha).
2. **The #3 reframe.** Does the move from "return" to "not alpha" land cleanly, or does it read as hedging? Is the distinction between the gross/SPY-excess return and the risk-adjusted alpha explained well enough for a reader who is not a factor-model specialist? Suggest tighter phrasing where the logic is muddy.
3. **The forthcoming corrections.** Two footnotes disclose corrections (the walk-down +$1.55 artifact; the implied-EPS reversal). Do they read as honest and matter-of-fact, or as defensive/apologetic? Are they in the right places?
4. **Jargon and density.** Flag sentences that are too long, too technical, or that gesture at a point without making it. The author specifically dislikes short fragments that assert without arguing and overlong clause-chains that lose the reader. Point to specific sentences and propose plainer rewrites.
5. **Diction and register.** Flag any remaining casual phrasing (second-person "you", colloquialisms, vivid-but-loose words like "hemorrhage", "bleed"), any remaining em-dashes, and any first-person-singular slips. Note: a single short declarative used for emphasis after a long passage is fine; a fragment that merely restates is not.
6. **Section flow.** Does the new "Out-of-Sample Validation" section (Section 6) earn its length, and does Table 10's four-panel structure (primary vs pooled, long/short vs short-only) read clearly? Is anything redundant between Section 4, Section 6, and the conclusion?
