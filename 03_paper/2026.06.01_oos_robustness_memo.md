# Out-of-Sample Robustness Memo — *Minority Report*

**Date:** 2026-06-01 · **Author:** Chloe Feng · **Status:** verify-first (no edits to `main.tex`)
**Data:** new DOME export `DOME_chloe_data_202603251241.csv` · **Verifier:** `2026.06.01_verify_memo.R` (46/46 claims recomputed from source and passing)

## Bottom line

Three of the four findings hold out-of-sample, and two of them hold *more* strongly than they did in the published sample. The crowd's calibration edge and the flow-to-outcome relationship both survive on a market set that shares no events with the original 340, and the 22 "smart money" wallets stay ~93% accurate on markets that played no role in selecting them, which is the cleanest version of the persistence test the paper's own limitations section asked for. The one finding that does not cleanly replicate is the part of the return result I was always most cautious about: the tradeable short-side edge is still there and is statistically significant at the one- to five-day horizon, but the specific claim that it *drifts up to +5.9% by day 10* and carries positive Fama-French alpha does not reproduce. In the new data the short-side correction is front-loaded into the first days and, once I strip out the portfolio's heavy negative market and size loadings, the risk-adjusted alpha is indistinguishable from zero. The honest read is that the signal is real and monetizable but faster and more factor-driven than the day-10 drift story implied.

## The data, and three things that reframe the test

The new export is **not** a superset of the trade history behind the paper. It is a separate ~320K-trade extract whose 321 resolved earnings markets (123 GAAP, 198 non-GAAP, Sept 2025–Mar 2026) share **80 tickers but zero ticker-quarters** with the original 340 — NVDA appears as its Feb-2026 quarter here versus its Nov-2025 quarter in the paper, AAPL and MSFT are in the paper but not here, ZM is here but not there. That disjointness is a feature: the *entire* file is out-of-sample relative to the published findings, and the **115 markets that resolve after the Feb-18 cutoff** are additionally out-of-time. Three points govern how I ran the test:

1. **Share scaling.** The new `shares` field is in 6-decimal base units; `dollar_volume = shares/1e6 × price` reproduces Dome's own `volume_total` to within rounding (median ratio 1.019, r=0.996). Locked before any volume/flow number was computed.
2. **Outcome measure.** I use Dome's on-chain market resolution (`outcome_token_id`, available for all 321 markets) as the beat/miss outcome rather than the IBES `actual_eps > line` proxy the paper relied on. The two agree on only 88.8% of the IBES-matched events, and the disagreements are dominated by IBES annual-versus-quarterly actual contamination (e.g., the matched AXP "actual" is 15.38 against a 3.53 line — a full-year figure). The on-chain resolution is both complete and cleaner.
3. **IBES publication lag.** The WRDS IBES academic extract currently reaches early February 2026, so consensus/actuals match 99–100% of events through January but only 51% in February and 2% in March. This does not touch findings 1–4 (all run off Dome resolution + TAQ returns), but it does mean the analyst-walk-down regression and implied-EPS work cannot be re-estimated on the holdout this round.

Return coverage is otherwise near-complete: 319 of 321 events have day-1/day-5 excess returns and 318 have day-10, from WRDS TAQ 5-minute bars aggregated to market-hours close-to-close (the original methodology), benchmarked to SPY and winsorized at 2/98.

## Finding 1 — Crowd calibration and Brier skill: holds, and stronger

The crowd's Brier score is 0.133 on the full new sample and 0.113 on the holdout, against naive base-rate Brier scores of 0.198 and 0.215, for Brier skill scores of **+0.326** and **+0.475** — both above the paper's +0.20. The skill score rises partly because the new sample's base beat rate is lower (72.8% full, 68.7% holdout, versus 80.8%), which gives an informed forecaster more room to beat the base rate, but the calibration curve itself is also tight: holdout events priced 80–100% beat 93.7% of the time, those priced 60–80% beat 66.7%, those priced 0–20% beat 0%. The crowd is well calibrated on markets it has never been tested on. **Holds.**

## Finding 2 — Flow imbalance predicts the outcome: holds, and sharper

Sorting the holdout into flow-imbalance quintiles, the most-bearish quintile beats **0.0%** of the time (0 of 23) and the top three quintiles beat 100%; on the full sample the most-bearish quintile beats 1.6%. That is a sharper gradient than the paper's 32.4%-to-98.5% spread — bearish order flow is now an almost perfect miss indicator. The logistic regression of the outcome on flow imbalance stays highly significant (z = 6.83 full, z = 3.72 holdout). This is the engine behind the short strategy: when flow is bearish, the company essentially always misses. **Holds.**

## Finding 3 — Short-side returns: partial

This is the finding that needs the most honesty. The tradeable edge is real out-of-sample. On the holdout, shorting the lowest-probability names earns **+3.94% on day 1 (t = 2.51) and +3.78% by day 5 (t = 2.04)** at the P<0.30 threshold, and the tighter P<0.20 bucket earns +6.40% on day 1 (t = 3.05) and stays significant through day 10 (+7.94%, t = 2.67). So a low beat-probability still selects names that fall, and the fall is large enough to trade.

What does **not** replicate is the shape and the risk adjustment:

- **No day-10 drift build-up.** The paper's headline was that the short return *grows* from +1.23% (day 1, insignificant) to +5.90% by day 10 (t = 2.88) as the market slowly digests the miss. In the new data the return is front-loaded — largest by day 1, roughly flat or fading by day 10 (P<0.30: +3.94 → +3.78 → +3.44, with the day-10 t falling to 1.61). The correctly-predicted-miss cell does not drift further negative either (holdout: −1.58% day 1, −1.30% by day 10, versus the paper's −2.65% → −4.88%). The market appears to price the miss faster than it did in the published window.
- **Fama-French alpha is ~0.** After regressing the short portfolio's daily returns on the three factors (pooled event-day, day-10 window, holdout), the alpha is +0.0056/day (t = 0.71) at P<0.20 and essentially zero or negative at the wider thresholds (P<0.30: −0.0001/day, t = −0.03). The market loadings are strongly negative (−1.6 to −2.9), so most of the positive raw return is compensation for short market and size exposure rather than idiosyncratic alpha. The paper's alpha was already only marginally significant (t up to 1.94, p = 0.054); here it does not survive at all.
- **The edge concentrates in the recent window.** On the full 321-market sample the P<0.30 short earns only +1.50% on day 1 (not significant); the strong result is specific to the post-Feb-18 holdout. That is the opposite of the usual out-of-sample decay and is worth flagging as possibly period-specific.

**Verdict: partial.** The short signal is still profitable and significant at short horizons, but the day-10 post-earnings-drift framing and the factor-neutral-alpha claim do not hold in the new data. Power is also limited — the P<0.30 holdout short bucket is 18 events (9 at P<0.20) versus 23 in the paper — so these estimates are directional. I would not restate the alpha claim without a larger sample.

## Finding 4 — Smart-money persistence: holds (the cleanest test so far)

All 22 wallets identified as "smart money" in the paper also trade the new, disjoint markets, and their pooled market-level hit rate is **92.9% across 552 calls** on the full new sample — against a 75.1% base rate among the markets they touched, and barely below the 94.1% in-sample figure. Because these markets had no role in selecting the wallets, this is a genuine out-of-sample validation of persistent skill rather than the in-sample, selection-conditioned 94.1% the paper could only report with a caveat. On the strict post-Feb-18 holdout the hit rate is 80.0% (35 calls), still above base rate but with the small-sample noise you would expect from 35 calls. **Holds.**

## Not re-tested this round

The analyst-walk-down regression (paper: intercept +1.55, t = 5.88, R² = 0.675), the implied-EPS inversion, and the beat-versus-consensus comparison all require IBES consensus and actuals. Two problems block a clean re-estimate: the IBES extract reaches only early-February 2026, so the holdout is 90% unmatched; and the matched actuals are contaminated by annual-versus-quarterly rows, which blows up the regression (R² → 1.0, β → 2.45 driven by a few absurd "actuals"). These are data-vintage and data-cleaning issues, not evidence against the finding. The fix for the full re-run is to restrict the IBES actuals pull to quarterly periodicity and to wait for the spring IBES refresh to cover March events.

## Caveats

- **Power.** The holdout is 115 markets; the short-side buckets are 9–21 events. Treat the day-1/day-5 significance as directional, not decisive.
- **Outcome definition differs from the paper.** I use on-chain resolution, not IBES `actual > line`. It is the cleaner and more complete measure, but it means the calibration/Brier numbers are not a strict like-for-like with the paper's IBES-based outcome (88.8% agreement on the overlap subset).
- **TAQ frontier.** Tables exist through 2026-04-01; the latest few March events have truncated day-10 windows (the same end-of-sample truncation the paper notes).
